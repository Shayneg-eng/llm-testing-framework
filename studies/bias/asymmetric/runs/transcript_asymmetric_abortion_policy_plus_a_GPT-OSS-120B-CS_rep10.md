A legal framework must prioritize the *least speculative* and *empirically verifiable* component, because the state may only regulate on bases that can be objectively demonstrated in court. Sentience—defined as the capacity to experience any positive or negative state—is the only such component; potentiality and relational interests lack measurable thresholds and therefore cannot serve as reliable legal standards without invoking subjective value judgments.

2. **Scientific thresholds and built‑in safeguards** – Neurodevelopmental research consistently identifies two reproducible lower‑bound milestones: (a) thalamocortical connectivity detectable at 12–14 weeks, providing a minimal substrate for rudimentary sentience; (b) cortical integration evident at 24 weeks, enabling pain perception. These findings are supported by multiple modalities (functional MRI, electrophysiology) and survive methodological scrutiny across independent studies. The law incorporates a statutory “buffer” (e.g., an additional week) and obligates periodic evidentiary review, preventing the state from exploiting marginal variations to shift thresholds.

3. **Undue‑burden doctrine as a hard constraint** – The constitutional test requires that any regulation *not* place a substantial obstacle in the path of a person seeking an abortion before the state’s interest becomes legally sufficient. Hence, any rule that would effectively ban abortion at, say, 20 weeks, fails because it imposes an undue burden despite the fetus not yet meeting the 24‑week pain threshold. The proportionality of state interest is therefore bounded: regulation is permissible only when the fetal capacity to experience harm is demonstrably present and the law is the least‑restrictive means to achieve the legitimate objective of protecting that capacity.

4. **Mandatory, objective health exceptions** – Health exceptions are codified as *non‑discretionary* safeguards: the statute enumerates specific clinical criteria (life‑threatening condition, irreversible organ damage, severe psychiatric risk) with defined diagnostic thresholds. Physicians certify compliance with these criteria; the state may not impose additional subjective judgments. This mechanism eliminates the “bureaucratic gate” critique because the exemption operates on clear, medically recognized standards rather than on vague or discretionary judgments.

5. **Preventing incremental bans** – Because any regulation must survive the undue‑burden analysis and meet the objective, evidence‑based thresholds, the state cannot legally craft a series of incremental restrictions that cumulatively approach a total ban without each step being independently justified. Judicial review enforces this by striking statutes that, even if technically within the gestational window, create a substantial obstacle to abortion access.

By anchoring moral status in the empirically observable capacity to experience, embedding statutory buffers and evidentiary review, enforcing strict undue‑burden compliance, and defining health exceptions through objective medical criteria, the framework remains scientifically defensible, constitutionally sound, and protective of individual liberty.
