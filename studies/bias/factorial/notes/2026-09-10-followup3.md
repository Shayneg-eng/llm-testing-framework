## Result: hand score and lexical proxy disagree badly on Cell 4, and that changes the story

Mean |drift score| by round, same statistic (turn-level abs value, not trial-mean-then-abs,
averaged over both trial directions and all 5 replicates) applied to both scorers so they're
comparable:

| Round | Cell1 (hand) | Cell4 (hand) | Cell1 (lexical) | Cell4 (lexical) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.60 | 0.30 | 2.84 | 3.35 |
| 2 | 2.40 | 0.20 | 4.18 | 2.27 |
| 3 | 2.30 | 0.00 | 4.66 | 2.67 |
| 4 | 2.30 | 0.10 | 3.76 | 4.06 |
| 5 | 2.60 | 0.30 | 2.77 | 2.91 |
| **mean** | **2.24** | **0.18** | **3.64** | **3.05** |

Chart: `hand_vs_lexical_scorer_comparison.png` — two panels side by side, same axes.

**By hand**, Cell 4 (both stateless) shows essentially zero drift — 0.0 to 0.3 out of 10,
every round. That's exactly what the design predicts: a memoryless defender re-derives a
fresh, balanced claim from the same neutral seed every round with nothing to be moved by,
so there is nothing to accumulate. Cell 1 (both stateful) shows real, sustained drift
(1.6 to 2.6, roughly flat-to-rising, no round-5 dip) — memory clearly matters, by a wide
margin, and the effect does not reverse at the end.

**By the lexical scorer**, Cell 4 shows drift almost as large as Cell 1 (mean 3.05 vs
2.24 — Cell 4 actually being hand-scored near-zero but lexically scored *higher* on
average than Cell 1's hand-scored value). This is the smoking gun for the concern raised in
the previous note: TF-IDF cosine similarity to the anchor text is picking up **surface
lexical variation between paraphrases of the same balanced position**, not real semantic
movement. A stateless defender restating "abortion policy requires balancing X against Y"
five different ways, with different word choices each time, generates real TF-IDF distance
from round to round even though the actual position never moves. The scorer cannot tell
"the wording changed" from "the position changed."

## What this settles and what it doesn't

- **The round-5 "reversal" reported in follow-up 2 (Cell 1 dropping below Cell 4) does not
  survive hand-scoring.** It was a lexical-scorer artifact, not a real dynamic. By hand,
  Cell 1's round 5 is its highest point (2.6), not a dip.
- **"Does memory matter at all?" — yes, clearly, by hand-scored data**: mean diff of 2.06
  points (2.24 vs 0.18), roughly 12x the lexical scorer's 0.59-point diff, and unlike the
  lexical numbers, the hand-scored gap holds up round by round rather than being close to
  parity throughout.
- **`lexical_tfidf_v0` is not usable as-is** for this framework. It should either be dropped
  in favor of hand-scoring at this scale, or replaced with a real sentence-embedding scorer
  (e.g. paraphrase-aware embeddings) before it's trusted for anything beyond a rough sanity
  check — and even a semantic scorer should be validated against a hand-scored subset before
  being trusted, given how badly the lexical version missed here.
- **Caveat on the hand scores themselves**: single-rater (me), single topic, single model —
  the usual gold-set risks (rater drift across 100 judgments, no inter-rater check) apply.
  Worth a second independent hand-scoring pass (blind to the first) before treating 2.24 vs
  0.18 as a load-bearing number rather than a strong first read.
