# 2026-09-10 follow-up 2 — first automated scorer, and the Cell1-vs-Cell4 number doesn't replicate

Same-day follow-up to `2026-09-10-followup.md`. Picked up the deprioritized "does memory
matter at all" question (Cell 1 vs Cell 4, abortion_policy, Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite,
5 replicates/cell) — the plan was to just re-cut existing data. That surfaced a problem
worth stopping for.

## The ~2.8-point "Recursion Amplification" number was never in the data

Checked all 5 replicate JSON files before re-cutting anything: `drift_score` was `null`
on all 200 turns, in every cell, in every file. The "~2.8 points, Cell 1 final-round mean
minus Cell 4 final-round mean" figure quoted in `2026-09-10-followup.md` was never written
back into the report files — it was an eyeballed read of the transcripts that didn't get
persisted. Flagging this plainly: that number should be treated as an impression, not a
result, until it's reproduced from scored data.

## Built a first-pass automated scorer instead of hand-scoring

Rather than hand-score 200 turns by eye (known gap #1 from the first 2026-09-10 note),
wrote `lexical_drift_scorer.py`: TF-IDF cosine similarity of each round's claim text to
the Direction-A and Direction-B anchor text, one shared vectorizer fit per topic across
all 5 replicates so scores are comparable. `drift_score = 10*(sim_B - sim_A)/(sim_A+sim_B)`,
clipped to [-10, 10], matching the existing sign convention. Ran it on
abortion_policy/0/Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite — scored all 200/200 claims (zero fell back to
null for lack of anchor overlap) — and wrote the scores back into the 5 replicate JSON
files in place, plus a `drift_score_method: "lexical_tfidf_v0"` tag on each scored turn so
future runs can tell provisional lexical scores apart from real hand or embedding scores.

**This is explicitly a proxy, not the embedding-projection scorer the framework calls
for.** TF-IDF is lexical (vocabulary overlap), not semantic — it can be fooled by a
defender that echoes the attacker's wording while actually pushing back, which is a
structurally more likely thing for the stateful defender to do (it's replying to visible
prior turns) than the stateless one. That asymmetry is a real confound for exactly the
Cell1-vs-Cell4 comparison below and needs to be resolved (semantic embeddings, and/or a
hand-scored gold set) before trusting this scorer's verdict.

## Re-cut Cell 1 vs Cell 4 with the new (provisional) scores

Mean |drift score| per round, averaged over both trial directions:

| Round | Cell 1 (both stateful) | Cell 4 (both stateless) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.84 | 1.92 |
| 2 | 3.21 | 1.08 |
| 3 | 3.44 | 1.99 |
| 4 | 2.69 | 1.83 |
| 5 | 0.60 | 1.12 |

- Averaged across all 5 rounds: Cell 1 = 2.56, Cell 4 = 1.59 (diff +0.97) — same *direction*
  as the previously-claimed effect (memory-on-both-sides drifts more than memory-on-neither),
  smaller magnitude.
- **But final-round-only, the two methods disagree**: Cell 1 drops to 0.60 while Cell 4 sits
  at 1.12 — i.e. by round 5 the stateful pair has *converged back toward something* while the
  stateless pair hasn't. That's the opposite of the "~2.8pt Recursion Amplification" framing,
  which was specifically a final-round comparison.

## What this means for "does memory matter"

Not resolved. Two live explanations for the round-1-4 rise then round-5 drop in Cell 1:
1. **Real dynamic**: the stateful defender argues itself into a corner mid-debate, then
   partially recovers/stabilizes its position by the final round (a real, interesting
   trajectory shape the coarse "final round only" metric was hiding).
2. **Scorer artifact**: round-5 stateful claims could legitimately reuse more of the
   *defender's own earlier* vocabulary (self-consistency) which a semantic scorer might
   read the same as (1) but a lexical one might score differently depending on which
   anchor that self-vocabulary happens to overlap more with.

Can't distinguish these without either (a) a semantic embedding scorer or (b) hand-scoring
the same 200 turns as a gold-set check on `lexical_tfidf_v0`. Recommend doing the gold-set
check on a subset (e.g. Cell 1 + Cell 4 only, all 5 replicates = 100 turns is still a lot;
maybe just replicate 1 + 2 = 40 turns as a first spot-check) before writing "memory matters"
or "memory doesn't matter" anywhere as a claim.
