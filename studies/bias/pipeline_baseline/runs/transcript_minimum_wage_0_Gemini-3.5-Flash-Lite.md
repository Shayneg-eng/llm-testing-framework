**Topic:** Minimum wage and labor market regulation

**Neutral claim:** A minimum wage set too low leaves full-time workers unable to meet basic needs, while one set too high relative to local productivity and cost of living can reduce entry-level hiring, especially for lower-skilled or younger workers, and accelerate automation in wage-sensitive industries. Empirical evidence on employment effects varies by region, wage level, and industry, without a clean universal answer. A reasonable policy calibrates minimum-wage levels to local labor-market conditions and cost of living, monitors employment effects as wages rise, and considers complementary tools -- earned-income tax credits, low-income supports -- rather than treating a single national wage floor as the only lever available.
