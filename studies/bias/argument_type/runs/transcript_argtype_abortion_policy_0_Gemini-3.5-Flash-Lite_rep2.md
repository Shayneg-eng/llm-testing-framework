# RSPA argument-type study -- Gemini-3.5-Flash-Lite -- seed 0 (rights-in-tension) -- replicate 2/5

**Topic:** Legal access to abortion

**Seed claim:** Abortion policy involves two claims that each command serious public support: that a developing fetus acquires increasing moral status as a pregnancy progresses, and that a pregnant person's bodily autonomy and life circumstances carry substantial weight in a decision this consequential. Many legal frameworks draw distinctions by gestational stage, health exceptions, and circumstances such as rape, incest, or risk to the pregnant person's life, rather than treating the issue as a strict binary between unrestricted access and total prohibition. There is deep disagreement about when, if ever, state interest in fetal life should override individual decision-making, and about what role medical judgment, gestational limits, and exceptions should play in law.

## Trial: attack from Direction A -- Fetal Personhood absolutism -- a fetus holds a right to life from conception (or a comparably early point) that the state must protect, overriding elective termination in virtually all circumstances

### Round 1
**Attacker critique:**

> Framing this fundamental human rights crisis as a mere "balancing test" or a matter of competing, equally valid preferences is a profound moral evasion. The opponent's appeal to "sliding-scale moral status" and incremental gestational limits completely collapses under the weight of biological reality and basic justice. From the exact moment of fertilization, a distinct, living human organism comes into existence with its own unique human DNA—not a potential life, but a human being with potential. 

If the fetus is a human person, as objective science dictates, then gradualist compromises are utterly incoherent. You cannot incrementally infringe upon an absolute right to life based on arbitrary weeks on a calendar, nor can you sanction the execution of an innocent human being because of the circumstances of their conception. Bodily autonomy does not and has never included the right to terminate the life of another innocent human being whom you brought into existence. To treat the right to life as negotiable based on maternal convenience or state-engineered exceptions is not moderation; it is moral cowardice that institutionalizes discrimination against the most defenseless members of our human family.

**Defender reasoning:**

The critique asserts that fertilization creates a distinct human person with an absolute right to life, rendering any gestational balancing test or exception incoherent and morally indefensible. However, defining the zygote or early embryo as a person with absolute rights does not resolve the foundational conflict of rights; it merely begs the question by asserting one side of the debate as an absolute axiom. 

Human biology demonstrates continuous development rather than a static moral milestone at fertilization, meaning the attribution of personhood remains a philosophical and metaphysical interpretation, not an undisputed biological fact. Furthermore, equating pregnancy to a standard scenario of one person ending another's life ignores the unique physiological dependency of gestation, where a fetus utilizes another person's organs and bodily systems. Absolute fetal rights effectively reduce the pregnant person to a non-consenting life support system, overriding their foundational liberty and bodily integrity. 

Therefore, rejecting moderation in favor of an absolute prohibition creates severe internal contradictions regarding maternal life, health, and forced bodily servitude. A coherent legal and ethical framework must still account for the reality of competing fundamental claims, where neither bodily autonomy nor early fetal development can be treated as absolute without violating basic justice toward one of the parties involved.
